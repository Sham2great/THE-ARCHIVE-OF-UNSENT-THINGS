RECORD 001 / EARLY YEARS
WANTED TO BE SEEN

The kid who just wanted to feel loved.

I grew up surrounded by family and friends, so from the outside, it probably looked like I had people around me. And I did. But being surrounded by people and feeling understood by them are two different things.

Somehow, even in a room full of people, I could still feel like everyone was against me. I couldn't always explain why. I just knew I wanted to feel chosen, safe, and loved without having to fight so hard to prove I deserved it.

Maybe that's where it started: not with wanting everybody to love me, but with wanting to know what it felt like when somebody really did.

RECORD 002 / PRIMARY SCHOOL
EVERYONE ELSE / SOMEWHERE ELSE

Trying to fit in, feeling like the odd one out.

Primary school was where I started noticing the distance between being around people and actually fitting in with them. I wanted to connect. I wanted to be part of things without feeling like I had to change something about myself first.

But it often felt like I was the odd one out, the person who didn't quite land where everybody else seemed to. When you're young, you don't always have the words for that feeling. You just start wondering if there's something about you that other people can see and you can't.

I kept trying anyway. I wanted to belong so badly that I didn't always stop to ask whether I was being accepted for who I was.

RECORD 003 / GRADES 7–8
✦✦✦MORE NOISE / SAME VOID

Trying to fill a void with attention.

At some point, I started talking to multiple people, thinking maybe the attention would fill the space I had been carrying for years. Maybe if enough people liked me, I would finally stop feeling like something was missing.

It didn't work. Attention could distract me for a while, but it couldn't give me the kind of connection I was actually looking for. So I made a decision: one person at a time. If I was going to love someone, I wanted to understand them, learn the small things about them, make them feel comfortable, and give them my all.

I stopped wanting a crowd. I wanted one person who meant it. I didn't know yet how much that choice would ask of me, or how differently each person would leave their mark.

“I didn't want more people. I wanted one person to feel like enough.”

RECORD 004 / DEV
✦MESSAGE NOT RECEIVED

Dev: the beginning that didn't make sense.

Dev seemed sweet and genuine at first. I believed in that version of her, and I tried to make sure she felt comfortable, even while I was dealing with my own problems. I put effort into understanding her and making room for what she needed.

Over time, though, communication became difficult, and emotional maturity felt missing when we needed it most. What hurt wasn't only the breakup. It was feeling like something I had worked so hard to protect could end without a reason I could understand.

I was left trying to make sense of an ending that didn't feel like it matched everything I had put into it. I couldn't force an explanation out of someone who wasn't able or willing to give me one. I could only sit with the questions.

RECORD 005 / TYRA
LOL✧♡A GOOD MOMENT CAN STILL END

Tyra: the calm I could laugh with.

Tyra felt different. There was a calmness to her, and a kind of ease that let us laugh together. For a while, it felt good to be with someone around whom joy could happen naturally.

But there were things that happened that didn't line up with what I wanted in someone I could imagine building a future with. I could care about a person and still recognize that caring didn't automatically make us right for each other.

That was its own kind of hurt: realizing that someone can bring you genuine good moments and still not be the person whose values and choices fit the life you're trying to build. Not every connection is fake just because it doesn't last. Not every good moment means you should stay.

RECORD 006 / JADA
✦✦✧✦WHAT I BELIEVED / WHAT I FOUND

Jada: the love I gave, and the truth I had to discover.

Jada mattered to me because I wasn't treating love like a game anymore. Back in grades 7 and 8, I had talked to different people trying to fill a void. By the time I chose to give myself to one person at a time, I wanted something real: loyalty, honesty, commitment, and the feeling that two people were choosing each other with the same seriousness. With Jada, I believed I was finally living by those values instead of just talking about them.

Then I found out there had been another person in the picture. It wasn't simply something from before us that had been left in the past. The connection had continued into our relationship, for much of the time we were together. There were replies and contact I didn't know about, and instead of getting the full truth from the start, I had to piece things together myself through the signs I noticed. The fact that I had to investigate my own relationship to understand what was happening changed the way I looked at everything.

What hurt wasn't only that something physical had happened. It was knowing there had also been an emotional connection, and realizing that the person I thought was choosing me had kept part of the truth somewhere I couldn't reach. I started looking back at memories, places, and moments that had once felt safe and seeing them differently. It made the past feel uncertain, as if I had to question which parts I had understood correctly and which parts I had been allowed to believe.

When we talked about it, I heard explanations about guilt, pity, feeling responsible for the other person, and not wanting to hurt him. I could understand that a situation might be complicated without pretending that those reasons made the secrecy harmless. Even if it wasn't love for him, the choices still happened. And even if I hadn't known the full story at the time, I was the one left trying to understand how the person I trusted could keep making those choices while we were together.

I wasn't perfect either, and I don't need to rewrite either of us as a monster to tell the truth about what it did to me. I wanted honesty before I had to ask for it. I wanted loyalty that didn't depend on whether I caught something. I wanted to feel that the person beside me was protecting what we had, not that I had to become suspicious just to know where I stood. Once that sense of safety broke, reassurance alone couldn't instantly put it back together.

Part of me could still imagine loving her again, but not by pretending nothing happened. If trust was ever going to be rebuilt, it would have to be through patience, consistency, transparency, and changed actions over time. I couldn't make myself feel safe just because I missed her, and forgiving someone wouldn't mean the betrayal suddenly became okay. I had to face the difference between wanting the relationship back and knowing what it would take for it to become healthy.

And then there was what all of it did to my view of love. Love had been one of the strongest parts of who I was. I prayed about what happened and kept trying to understand it, but for months I felt something in me going quiet. My values around commitment, intimacy, and what it means to give yourself to someone still mattered, yet it became harder to believe that another person would hold those things with the same care. The part that frightened me was not only losing Jada. It was noticing that something I had always believed in so deeply no longer felt the same inside me.

“I thought the hardest thing would be losing her. I didn't expect to lose the certainty I had in love, too.”

RECORD 007 / THE PRESENT
♨♥FEELING, AT LOW VOLUME

When the feeling started going quiet.

Now, there's a numbness I don't fully know how to explain. It's not that I suddenly decided love means nothing, or that everything I believed in was a lie. It's more like the certainty I used to have has started fading, and I don't recognize that change in myself.

I've spent so much time wanting real commitment, loyalty, understanding, and a kind of love that isn't reduced to something sexual. So when it feels like the people I meet want something different from what I want, it leaves me wondering where the kind of connection I believe in is supposed to fit.

I can miss being hopeful without being able to feel hopeful right now. I can still care about love and be tired of searching for it. I don't have to force an answer tonight, and I don't have to pretend the things that happened didn't change me.
